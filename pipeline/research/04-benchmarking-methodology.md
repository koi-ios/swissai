# Benchmarking and measurement methodology for small-n LLM-serving cold-start and latency experiments

Compiled 12 Nov 2026. Ledger IDs B01 to B28. Every claim outside the ledger cites an ID. Quotes are under 15 words; everything else is paraphrase. Primary PDFs were opened and searched as text for B01 to B07, B09, B10, B11, B13 to B19 and B26. B08 and B21 to B25 and B27 are web pages, read directly. B12 and B20 were not opened (see Not verified).

## Bottom line

1. **The workspace rule "median of at least 3 runs with min to max" is fine for describing results, but it does not support a claim that A beats B.** If runs are i.i.d., the min-to-max range of n runs contains the true median with probability 1 − 2·0.5^n. That is 75% at n = 3, 87.5% at n = 4, 93.8% at n = 5 and 96.9% at n = 6 (B28). B01 likewise says a nonparametric CI needs n > 5. With 3 independent runs per arm, even complete separation gives a two-sided Mann-Whitney p of 0.10 (B28). Replace the rule with: **n = 3 is descriptive only; n ≥ 6 per arm, or ≥ 6 interleaved pairs, before any comparative claim.** Confidence: high (derived statistics). IDs: B01, B06, B07, B28.

2. **Use a paired, interleaved design (ABAB, or A/B in random order within each block), with the container or host as the block.** On shared cloud hardware, between-host and between-time variance dominate. Interleaving cancels drift, and pairing within a block removes host effects. Analyse the per-pair ratio. With 6 pairs that all point the same way, the two-sided sign test gives p = 0.031; with 5 pairs it gives 0.0625 (B28). Confidence: high for the principle (B06 §7.1, §7.5; B07; B04). Confidence: medium that 6 pairs are enough when noise is heavy-tailed.

3. **Run an A/A pilot (baseline against itself, n = 5 to 6 interleaved) before any A/B run, and report the noise floor.** Claim an effect only if it clearly exceeds the A/A spread, and say so in the write-up. Pilots are how B06's CONFIRM, B04 and B01 (§4.2.2) size n. B06 shows the required n swings from 10 runs (CoV 0.3%) to 240 runs (CoV 9%) for a 1%-wide CI. The noise has to be measured on the actual Modal or cluster setup; it cannot be assumed. Confidence: high. IDs: B01, B04, B06, B07.

4. **Report the median with all raw points (strip or dot plot) and min to max. Report the effect as the ratio of medians plus the range of paired ratios, not as a mean ± SD and not as best-of-n.** Performance data are usually non-normal: B06 rejected normality for most servers, and B01 Rule 6 says not to assume it. Best-of-n misled in up to 16% of comparisons in B03. Report absolute times alongside ratios (B01 Rule 1, B08). Confidence: high. IDs: B01, B03, B06, B08, B09.

5. **Treat cache and environment state as an experimental factor, not as noise. Record it and state it per run.** Factors include page cache (warm or cold), model-weight location (volume, local disk, RAM), torch.compile and Triton caches, CUDA-graph or JIT caches, image layer cache, and host and GPU ID. B16 found disabling the compile cache raised one phase from about 3–6 s to 11–21 s. B16 ran most experiments with a warm buffer cache and repeated them after flushing it. B15 cleared page and inode caches and randomised over 20 checkpoint copies. Where the page cache cannot be dropped, as on shared containers, label results "warm page cache" or force cold reads another way, and say which. Confidence: high. IDs: B05, B02, B15, B16, B19.

6. **Measure phase breakdowns with harness-side monotonic timestamps. Do not rely on server log lines, and define "ready" as the first successful generated token from a real request.** B16 warns that vLLM's log granularity can add up to hundreds of ms of error. Leading cold-start papers report per-phase breakdowns (B16, B19). Confidence: medium (the definition of "ready" is a convention, not derived). IDs: B16, B19, B27.

7. **For latency under load, use open-loop arrivals (Poisson or gamma at a finite rate) and report percentiles only when there are enough requests behind them.** Closed loops hide stalls, which is coordinated omission (B22). The YCSB example in B22 shows a closed-loop P99 in µs while the intended-time P99 was about 665 ms. MLPerf requires ≥ 600 s and on the order of 10^4–10^5 queries for tail percentiles (B21). A student budget therefore supports medians and P90 over a few hundred requests, but not credible P99s. Confidence: high. IDs: B21, B22, B24, B25, B26.

8. **Gate every performance patch on a correctness-equivalence check.** Use greedy output equality on a fixed prompt set at a fixed batch size, plus a log-probability tolerance. Document that non-batch-invariant kernels make outputs depend on load: B23 found 80 distinct completions out of 1,000 at temperature 0. An exact-match test must therefore pin the batch size or concurrency, or use batch-invariant mode, which cost 1.6–2.1× in B23. MLPerf uses accuracy thresholds of 99% or 99.9% of the reference instead of exact match (B21). Confidence: medium-high. IDs: B21, B23.

9. **Label every number MEASURED, PREDICTED or ESTIMATE. A PREDICTED number must name its model and its held-out validation error.** B16's per-step linear predictor was trained on some models and validated on 5 held-out models (MSE 2.42 s). BlitzScale motivates its design with a simulator built on DistServe before measuring the real system (B17). Simulation is acceptable for motivation and sizing, and must be labelled (B08 crime "simplified simulated system"). Confidence: high. IDs: B08, B16, B17.

10. **Spend the GPU budget on the cheapest decisive experiment, and repeat at the level where variance per dollar is highest.** Write the hypothesis, the predicted effect and the stopping rule down before running. B04 shows the optimal repetition count per level depends on that level's variance and cost. For cold starts, repetition must happen at the process or container level, because repeating within one process measures warm state (B03, B04). More runs are not automatically better: in B07, cloud token buckets made CIs widen with more repetitions, and 80% of TPC-DS queries ended up with medians more than 10% off. Confidence: medium. IDs: B03, B04, B07, B01.

**Proposed protocol for n = 3 to 10 (replaces the current rule):**

- **Before running.** Write one line stating the hypothesis, the predicted effect size, the decision rule ("claim if the ratio of medians is beyond the A/A range and ≥ 5 of 6 pairs agree") and the maximum n. Commit it with a timestamp (B01 Rule 9 spirit; B04).
- **Pilot (A/A).** Run baseline against baseline, 6 interleaved fresh-process runs in one container, then repeat in a second container if affordable. Record the CoV and the range. If the CoV is above about 10%, find the noise source before going further (B06 treats ≤ 10% CoV as normal hardware variability).
- **Main run.** Use 6 pairs by default (n = 6 per arm, 12 cold starts), with ABAB or random order within each pair. Each run is a fresh process. Use a new container per block where the cost allows. Drop nothing silently; if a run is excluded, report it and the reason (B01 on outliers, B08).
- **Escalation.** Go to n = 10 only if the effect is within 2× the A/A range. Stop at the pre-declared maximum n. If the effect is still ambiguous, report it as "not distinguishable from noise at n = 10", which is a legitimate null result (B05).
- **n = 3.** Allowed only for exploration and smoke tests, for effects many times larger than the noise. Report all 3 values with "n = 3, descriptive, no significance claim."
- **What to report.** All raw values; the median and min to max per arm; the ratio of medians; the range of paired ratios; the A/A noise floor; and the sign-test p only at n ≥ 6. For load tests, report TTFT and TPOT medians and P90 with the request count, open-loop rate and burstiness (B24).
- **What to record per run (a JSON line).** UTC timestamp; host and GPU UUID; GPU model, driver and CUDA version; container image digest; engine commit hash and version; torch version; model repository revision; cache states (page cache, compile cache, CUDA-graph cache: hit or miss, path); weight storage location; phase timestamps; and the exit status. Release the raw JSONL and a script that regenerates every figure (B01 Rule 9, B08, B09).
- **Labels.** Use MEASURED (n, setup, date), PREDICTED (model, validation error) or ESTIMATE (basis).

## Disagreements

**Minimum versus median versus mean.**
- *Minimum:* Chen and Revels argue that timing noise is positive and additive, that the median and mean are bimodal across trials, and that the minimum is a stable estimator of the true cost (B10).
- *Against best-of-n:* Georges et al. show best-of-30 misleads in up to 16% of comparisons, because it ignores nondeterminism (B03).
- *Median and percentiles:* Hoefler argues for these because the data are non-normal and the question decides the statistic, e.g. tails for latency (B01 Rules 6 and 8). Maricq et al. agree (B06).
- *Fit for this setting:* the median. A cold start's noise is not purely additive. A "fast" outlier can be a run that silently hit a warm page cache or compile cache, which is a different regime, not less noise (B05, B16). The minimum would then report the wrong condition. The minimum is defensible only for in-process microbenchmarks of a single kernel or phase, as in B10's setting.

**Mean with a parametric CI versus median with a nonparametric CI.**
- *Mean-based:* Georges (t-CI on the mean, B03), Kalibera and Jones (effect-size CI via ANOVA, B04) and Stabilizer (randomise until approximately normal, then t-test, B05) work with means.
- *Median-based:* Hoefler and Maricq reject normality for most real data (B01, B06).
- *Fit for this setting:* at n = 6–10 without a layout-randomisation mechanism, normality cannot be checked. So: medians, order-statistic CIs and the sign test (B28). B04's effect-size framing ("A is X% ± Y% faster") is still the right way to phrase a result, via paired ratios.

**How many runs.**
- *Few runs:* Heiser says "several runs" and expects SD < 0.1% on dedicated hardware (B08). Leading cold-start papers use 5 runs averaged (B16) or 20 copies averaged (B15), and some state no count at all (B17, B18, B19, found by text search only). On this evidence, top venues currently accept n = 5 means for cold start.
- *Many runs:* Hoefler requires n > 5 for a nonparametric CI (B01). Maricq finds 10 to 240 runs for a 1%-wide CI depending on CoV (B06). Uta finds 70+ runs in clouds (B07). MLPerf needs tens to hundreds of thousands of queries for tail percentiles (B21).
- *Reconciliation:* the large counts target a 1%-wide CI. A cold-start patch that claims a 1.5–10× gain only needs to separate distributions, not pin the median to 1%. So n = 6 interleaved pairs suffices when the effect is ≫ the A/A range; small effects (< 2× the A/A range) honestly need B06-scale n, which the budget cannot buy. Report those as inconclusive.

**Bootstrap at small n.**
- *For:* Kalibera and Jones offer a bootstrap alternative for effect-size CIs (B04).
- *Against:* Hesterberg shows percentile bootstrap CIs are too narrow for small n, especially for the median (B13).
- *Fit for this setting:* no bootstrap at n ≤ 10. Use exact order statistics (B28).

**Simulation and prediction.**
- *Against:* Heiser lists the "simplified simulated system" as a crime unless representativeness is shown (B08).
- *In practice:* BlitzScale uses a simulator for motivation (B17), and Breaking the Ice publishes a predictor validated on held-out models (B16).
- *Fit for this setting:* predictions are acceptable when labelled and validated on held-out cases (D9).

## Disconfirming evidence and null results

- **Against the workspace rule (median of ≥ 3, min to max).** At n = 3 the min-to-max range covers the true median only 75% of the time, even under i.i.d. (B28). In emulation, 3-run medians fell outside the 50-run 95% CI in 6 of 8 network distributions, and 10-run medians in 3 of 8 (B07). Three runs cannot support a CI at all (B01, B07). The rule is defensible only as a description, and for effects much larger than the noise floor.
- **Against "more runs are always better".** Repetition within one setup does not remove setup bias: link order and environment size bias the result regardless of the number of runs (B02, B05). In clouds, token buckets made repeated runs dependent, so CIs widened as n grew (B07). SSD state carried over across weeks (B06). Benchmark order changed STREAM by 3× (B06). Under a fixed budget, spreading runs across blocks (containers, hosts, times) beats piling them into one (B04, B06).
- **Null results as legitimate findings.** Stabilizer found -O3 versus -O2 indistinguishable from noise once layout was randomised (B05). This shows a well-designed null is publishable and that apparent small gains can be bias.
- **Against D4 (median).** For microbenchmarks under additive noise, the minimum is more stable than the median (B10). This counter-evidence is real, but out of scope for whole cold starts.
- **Against the claim that top venues demand rigorous statistics.** The leading cold-start papers examined report means of 5 or 20 runs or give no count, and text search found no error bars (B15, B16, B17, B18, B19). In practice, a write-up meeting the protocol above would exceed that norm. The risk is in being sloppy, not in falling short of the norm.
- **Against exact-match correctness checks.** Greedy outputs at temperature 0 are not reproducible under varying load without batch-invariant kernels: 80 unique outputs out of 1,000 (B23). A naive exact-match test will flake. Pin the batch size or concurrency, or use a tolerance.
- **Heterogeneity of same-SKU GPUs.** Same-SKU GPUs differ by 8% on average and up to 1.5× (B14). A/B runs on different GPUs are therefore confounded unless blocked.

## Transfer notes

- **Transfers directly (cheap):**
  - Interleaving and order randomisation (B06 §7.1).
  - A/A noise floor (B01, B04, B06).
  - Nonparametric order-statistic CIs and the sign test (B01, B28).
  - Recording the environment and releasing raw data (B01 Rule 9, B08, B09).
  - Open-loop load (B22, B24).
  - Correctness gates (B21, B23).
  - Labelling predictions (B16).

  None of these cost GPU time beyond the runs themselves.
- **Transfers with changes:**
  - Cache control: B15 and B16 flush the page cache with root on dedicated nodes. On shared containers the page cache usually cannot be dropped. Substitutes:
    - Treat warm/cold as a labelled factor.
    - Use a fresh container or volume per cold run.
    - Read weights once to deliberately warm them and compare against that.
    - State "warm page cache" as in B16's default.
  - Blocking: a dedicated testbed (B06, B14) can block by named server. On Modal-style shared GPUs, record the GPU UUID and host ID per run and treat each container as a block (B14).
- **Does not transfer:**
  - Heiser's expectation of SD < 0.1% (B08) assumes dedicated, quiet hardware. The cloud CoV of 0.3–29% (B06) and noisy neighbours (B06 §7.5, B07) make this unrealistic.
  - MLPerf-scale runs of ≥ 600 s and 10^4–10^5 queries (B21) are unaffordable on a ~$90 budget for tail percentiles. Report P50 and P90 and say why P99 is omitted.
  - B06-style counts of 70–240 runs for 1% CIs (B06, B07) are unaffordable; restrict claims to large effects.
- **Cluster runs (university lab).** These resemble B18's setting: a shared cluster with exclusive node allocation. Use them for the decisive n = 6–10 paired runs. Use Modal for pilots and smoke tests (B06, B18).

## What better models change, and what they don't

- **Change:** frontier coding agents make the full protocol cheap in human hours. They can generate the per-run JSONL recorder, the interleaving and randomisation harness, figure-regeneration scripts and the order-statistic and sign-test analysis. That removes the main historical excuse for the under-reporting documented in B01, B04, B07 and B09.
- **Change:** they also raise the risk of confident fabrication. Agents may summarise papers inaccurately (B12 and B20 could not be verified here), or produce numbers without provenance. The MEASURED / PREDICTED / ESTIMATE labels and raw-data release (D9) guard against this.
- **Don't change:** the statistics. Coverage of min to max at n = 3 is 75% regardless of tooling (B28). Setup bias is not reduced by repetition (B02, B05). Cloud dependence breaks i.i.d. (B07). These hold across model and engine generations.
- **Generation-dependent:** engine-specific findings, such as phase shares of vLLM startup (B16: more than 4× variation across 9 releases), Foundry's warm-cache assumption (B19) and TPOT definitions (B24), must be re-measured per engine version. Always record the engine commit.
