# Research behind the doctrine

Compiled 12 Nov 2026 for [DOCTRINE.md](DOCTRINE.md). The question: how should one person run ML-systems research end to end while leaning on AI as much as possible, with about 15 hours a week, frontier coding agents, no local GPU, about $90 of Modal credit and cluster runs only through a lab?

The tables marked "generated" are rebuilt by `python3 pipeline/build_research.py`, which also fails if the doctrine cites an ID that no ledger has. Everything else on this page is hand-written.

## How this was produced, and its limits

- Eleven research agents ran in parallel, one topic each. Each was told to prefer primary sources, date and grade every source, look for disconfirming evidence, reason about transfer to a solo student, and say why it stopped. Their notes are in [research/](research/). Every note has its own ledger, disagreements, null results, transfer notes and a list of what it could not verify.
- The coordinating session read all eleven notes and wrote the doctrine and the hand-written parts of this page. The ledger below is copied from the notes by script, so no row was retyped.
- Grades: **A** controlled study (RCT or controlled experiment); **B** measured case study with a stated method; **C** practitioner report; **D** vendor claim; **E** opinion. Policy texts, documentation, terms of service and local checks carry no A to E grade. They are authoritative for what a project requires or a tool does, and say nothing about benefit.

Limits to weigh before trusting a decision:

1. **The search quota ran out.** The session's shared web-search quota was used up while the agents worked. Nine of the eleven notes list searches they could not run; the other two (06 and 08) list primary pages they could not reach. The gaps that matter most:
   - 2026 discussion at HotOS, OSDI, SOSP and NSDI of AI-generated systems results;
   - reward-hacking and false-success rates for 2026 models;
   - 2026 randomised trials of agentic tools other than METR's;
   - any study of how volume, depth or AI use affects research-assistant hiring;
   - practitioner reports on instruction-file drift and on handoff files;
   - Lustre against other storage for model loading;
   - SGLang maintainers' comments on AI-assisted pull requests.
2. **Some pages were read second-hand.** Many pages came through a summarising fetch tool, and some primary pages were blocked (X, acm.org, several vendor posts). Each note marks those rows UNVERIFIED. The section "Cited rows that rest on secondary reading" lists the ones the doctrine leans on.
3. **Some topics have no controlled evidence at all:** experiment operations (no grade A source), compaction against handoff files, the throughput of several supervised agents after review, writing a prediction before a run, and how supervisors perceive a student's AI use.
4. **Practitioner profiles are mostly C, D or E.** They record what credible people say they do. Almost none of it was measured.

## Disconfirming evidence that changed a decision

- **The workspace's own measurement rule.** With three runs, min to max contains the true median 75% of the time, and three against three cannot go below p = 0.10 (B28). On emulated cloud networks, three-run medians fell outside the 50-run interval in 6 of 8 cases (B07). Result: D6 replaces the rule.
- **My lifecycle guess.** The researcher profiles point to a smoke test on real hardware and a reproduced baseline before a harness is frozen, and to kill criteria written in advance (R22, R01, R04). Result: D1 adds them.
- **"The instruction file is the real asset."** The one controlled study found no significant gain in success and about 20% more cost (E18). It also found no relation between file length and success, so "short" in D8 is a cost rule, not a finding (C26). Result: D8 keeps the file small and moves rules into checks.
- **Evaluator agents.** No controlled comparison supports them against self-review on frontier coding agents; the vendor's own evaluator was lenient until tuned (H14). Result: D5 makes the reviewer advisory, behind deterministic checks.
- **Rewriting a handoff file every session, and compacting at a fixed fill level.** No measurement behind either schedule, and vendors reversed themselves (H15, H16). A status file at boundaries does have support (C34, R24). Result: D9 keeps one status file, updated when the state changes, and adopts no fixed ritual.
- **Idea tournaments and councils.** No study shows that ranking picks good directions; executed AI ideas scored below expert ideas (L14). The note rates this medium-low. Result: dropped as ways to choose direction. The other idea mechanisms in the playbooks were not evaluated by any note.
- **Removing credentials is not enough.** The security note rates default-deny egress and whole-process isolation for unattended runs as high confidence (S07, S08). The audit found the first draft of D11 had left them out. Result: D11 and D7 now require them.
- **Nine projects at once.** No source supports it; several point the other way (R26, M26). Result: D2.
- **Three accounts.** The credit is monthly, and using other people's accounts is the clearly risky case (X01, X02). Result: D10 plans on one.
- **`just`.** Nothing measured either way, and it is not installed. Result: `make`.

Searched for and not found: a study showing that several supervised agents raise output after review; a code-review study showing that disclosure lowers the odds of a merge; a documented case of a laptop agent leaking research through a cloned repository; a controlled comparison of compaction and handoff files; any measurement of writing predictions before runs.

## Corrections after the audit

The ledger below is copied from the notes unchanged. Where the audit found a row wrong or stale, the correction is here:

- **H21, L27** (ImpossibleBench). The paper gives 92%, not 93%, for one model under a loose prompt, falling to 1% under a strict one. It gives no figure for Opus 4.1 with an abort option, so "stayed at 46%" is unverified. E20 has the verified numbers.
- **H08.** The row describes version 2 of the paper (180 configurations). Version 3, of April 2026, reports 260 and no longer states the 45% threshold or the 17.2 times figure in its abstract. The direction is unchanged: up to 70% worse on sequential tasks.
- **E19 and C28** are one paper with two grades. It is a paired comparison of agent runs with and without the file, on 124 pull requests; it measured runtime and tokens, not success.
- **E23.** The row's "no drop up to about four projects a week" comes from a summary, not the paper.
